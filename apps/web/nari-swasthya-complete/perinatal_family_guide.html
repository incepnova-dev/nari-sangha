Evidence-Based Nutrition & Wellness

Targeted nutritional support and lifestyle interventions that directly impact brain chemistry and mood regulation.

Critical Nutrients

  • Omega-3 (EPA >1000mg): Improves neurotransmitter function
  • Vitamin D3: Target serum levels 40-60 ng/mL
  • Magnesium: Reduces anxiety and aids sleep

Daily Support

  • Protein + Complex Carbs: Stabilizes blood sugar
  • Hydration: Combats fatigue and headaches
  • Regular Snacks: Prevents "hangry" mood dips

Sleep Hygiene

  • Protect a 4-hour sleep block (Partners: take this shift!)
  • Reduce evening blue light exposure
  • Sleep when baby sleeps (actually do it)

Disease Trajectory

Understanding the natural progression helps identify intervention points and predict outcomes.

DAYS 3-10 POSTPARTUM

Baby Blues

Affects 50-80% of mothers. Tearfulness, mood swings, anxiety. Usually self-limiting.

2+ WEEKS POSTPARTUM

Perinatal Depression Onset

Symptoms persist beyond the "blues" window. Interferes with daily functioning and bonding.

URGENT / EMERGENCY

Severe Symptoms / Psychosis

Intrusive thoughts, detachment from reality, or risk of harm requires immediate ER visit.

When to Consult a Provider

Clear guidelines on urgency levels help ensure timely intervention.

IMMEDIATE (Within 24 Hours)
  • Suicidal thoughts or plan
  • Thoughts of harming the baby
  • Hallucinations or delusions
URGENT (Within 48-72 Hours)
  • Worsening symptoms affecting daily tasks
  • Severe insomnia not related to baby
  • Panic attacks
ROUTINE (Book Appointment)
  • Persistent low mood > 2 weeks
  • Difficulty bonding
  • Feeling overwhelmed constantly

IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED

If you notice suicidal thoughts, psychosis, or thoughts of harm, do not wait.

6 Powerful Support Strategies

Hover over each card to flip and discover actionable ways to help

1

Listen Without Judgment

Hover to see what to say & what NOT to say

What to Say & Not Say

✅ DO SAY:

  • "I'm here for you. You're not alone."
  • "This is a medical condition, not your fault."
  • "You're a good mother going through a hard time."
  • "What do you need right now?"

❌ DON'T SAY:

  • "Just snap out of it"
  • "Other moms have it worse"
  • "You should be grateful"
  • "It's just hormones"
2

Provide Practical Help

Hover for concrete actions

Concrete Actions

Don't ask "What can I do?"—Just DO it:
  • Take the 2 AM feeding shift
  • Prepare meals and keep water nearby
  • Do laundry, dishes, cleaning
  • Handle diaper changes and bathing
  • Watch baby so she can nap
  • Manage bills and paperwork

Depression makes decisions overwhelming. Just act.

3

Facilitate Treatment

Hover for action steps

Get Professional Help

  • Research perinatal mental health specialists
  • Schedule appointments for her
  • Provide transportation to sessions
  • Attend appointments if invited
  • Support medication without judgment
  • Handle insurance and paperwork

Treatment Options: Therapy (CBT, IPT), Medication, Support Groups, Couples Therapy

4

Protect Recovery Time

Hover to learn how

Create Safe Space

  • Manage visitors—screen calls, limit guests
  • Encourage self-care: "Go take a shower"
  • Treat therapy time as non-negotiable
  • Buffer her from judgmental family/friends
  • Celebrate small wins: "You got dressed today!"
  • Create quiet, peaceful home environment
5

Monitor & Advocate

Hover for tracking tips

Track Progress

  • Keep a symptom log to share with doctors
  • Use Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale
  • Report worsening symptoms immediately
  • Be patient—recovery takes 6+ months
  • Trust your instincts about her wellbeing
  • Communicate regularly with her healthcare team

Medications need 4-6 weeks to work. Stay vigilant.

6

Take Care of Yourself

Hover for self-care guidance

You Can't Pour From Empty Cup

Supporting someone with depression is exhausting. Your wellbeing matters too.

  • Find your own support group
  • Maintain your exercise routine
  • Accept help from others
  • Consider therapy for yourself
  • Take breaks when needed
  • Watch for caregiver burnout

⚠️ If you feel resentful, exhausted, or depressed, seek help immediately.

Your Support Saves Lives

Recovery from perinatal depression takes time—typically 6 months to a year with treatment. There will be good days and difficult days. Your patience, understanding, and active support are essential to her recovery.

"She is not weak. She is not a bad mother. She is ill. And with proper treatment and support, she WILL recover. Your love and advocacy make an immeasurable difference."

You don't need to be perfect. You just need to be present, compassionate, and willing to help.

Recovery is Possible

Perinatal depression is a medical condition, not a personal failing. With appropriate treatment and your support, she can heal and thrive.
