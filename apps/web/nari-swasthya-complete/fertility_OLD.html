Understanding Your Menstrual Cycle

Knowledge is power when planning for conception.

Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5)

Your period begins. The uterine lining sheds. Hormone levels are at their lowest.

Follicular Phase (Days 1-13)

Follicles in ovaries mature. Estrogen rises, thickening the uterine lining.

Ovulation (Day 14)

Fertile window! Egg is released. Best time for conception is 2-3 days before and day of ovulation.

Luteal Phase (Days 15-28)

Progesterone rises, preparing for pregnancy. If no conception, hormone levels drop and cycle repeats.

Fertility Tracking Methods

Multiple approaches to identify your fertile window.

Calendar Method

Track cycle length for 6+ months. Fertile window is typically days 10-17 of a 28-day cycle.

Easy to start

Basal Body Temperature (BBT)

Take temperature first thing each morning. Slight rise (0.5-1°F) indicates ovulation occurred.

Most accurate

Cervical Mucus Monitoring

Check consistency daily. Clear, stretchy, egg-white mucus indicates peak fertility.

No equipment needed

Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs)

Urine tests detect LH surge 24-48 hours before ovulation. Test daily during fertile window.

Most convenient

Fertility Apps

Digital tracking combines multiple methods. Predictions improve over time with data.

Comprehensive

Fertility Monitors

Advanced devices measure hormones or detect fertile saliva patterns for precise prediction.

Higher cost

Optimizing Your Fertility

Lifestyle factors that support conception.

Nutrition

  • Folic acid: 400-800 mcg daily
  • Antioxidants: berries, nuts, leafy greens
  • Healthy fats: avocado, olive oil, fish
  • Limit processed foods and trans fats

Healthy Weight

  • BMI 18.5-24.9 optimal for fertility
  • Underweight or overweight affects ovulation
  • Gradual, sustainable changes best
  • Focus on overall health, not just numbers

Exercise

  • Moderate activity 30 min most days
  • Improves circulation and hormone balance
  • Avoid excessive intense exercise
  • Yoga and walking excellent choices

Avoid Toxins

  • No smoking or secondhand smoke
  • Limit alcohol consumption
  • Reduce caffeine (under 200mg/day)
  • Minimize environmental toxins

Stress Management

  • High stress can affect ovulation
  • Practice meditation or mindfulness
  • Adequate sleep (7-9 hours)
  • Consider counseling or support groups

Supplements

  • Prenatal vitamin before conception
  • CoQ10 may improve egg quality
  • Vitamin D if deficient
  • Consult doctor before adding supplements

When to See a Fertility Specialist

Know when to seek professional help.

Consult a Specialist If:

  • Under 35: Trying for 12+ months without success
  • Over 35: Trying for 6+ months without success
  • Irregular periods: Cycles shorter than 21 or longer than 35 days
  • Known conditions: PCOS, endometriosis, thyroid disorders
  • Previous miscarriages: Two or more pregnancy losses
  • Male factor concerns: Partner has fertility issues
  • Medical history: Pelvic surgery, cancer treatment, STIs

What to Expect at First Visit:

  • Medical history: Both partners' health backgrounds
  • Physical exam: Pelvic exam and general health assessment
  • Blood tests: Hormone levels (FSH, LH, estrogen, progesterone, AMH)
  • Ultrasound: Check ovaries and uterus
  • Semen analysis: For male partner
  • Discussion: Treatment options based on findings

Fertility Treatment Options

Understanding available assisted reproductive technologies.

Ovulation Induction

Medications (Clomid, Letrozole) stimulate ovulation for women with irregular cycles or PCOS.

Success rate: 20-25% per cycle

IUI (Intrauterine Insemination)

Sperm placed directly into uterus during ovulation. Often combined with ovulation drugs.

Success rate: 10-20% per cycle

IVF (In Vitro Fertilization)

Eggs retrieved, fertilized in lab, embryos transferred to uterus. Most effective treatment.

Success rate: 30-50% per cycle (age-dependent)

ICSI

Single sperm injected into egg. Used with IVF for male factor infertility.

Success rate: Similar to IVF

Egg/Embryo Freezing

Preserve fertility for future use. Recommended before cancer treatment or to delay pregnancy.

Storage: Indefinite preservation

Donor Options

Egg, sperm, or embryo donation for those who cannot use own gametes.

Success rate: Varies by method
