Preparing for Your Screening

Tips for accurate results and smooth appointments.

Scheduling Tips

  • Book Pap smear mid-cycle (not during period)
  • Morning appointments best for fasting tests
  • Avoid deodorant before mammogram
  • Schedule colonoscopy when you can prep at home

What to Bring

  • Insurance card and photo ID
  • List of current medications
  • Family health history
  • Previous test results for comparison

Fasting Requirements

  • 8-12 hours: Cholesterol, glucose tests
  • Water okay: Stay hydrated
  • Medications: Ask doctor about continuing
  • No fasting: Pap smear, mammogram, bone density

Questions to Ask

  • Why is this test recommended for me?
  • When will I get results?
  • What do the results mean?
  • What are my next steps if abnormal?

📊 Understanding Your Pap Smear Results

✅

Normal (Negative)

No abnormal cells detected

Next Steps: Continue routine screening every 3 years (or 5 years with HPV co-test). Maintain healthy habits.
⚠️

ASC-US / LSIL

Atypical cells of uncertain significance

Next Steps: HPV test recommended. If HPV positive, colposcopy may be needed. Often resolves on its own. Follow-up in 12 months.
🔴

HSIL / Abnormal

High-grade precancerous changes

Next Steps: Colposcopy and biopsy required. Treatment may include LEEP or cryotherapy. Early detection allows 95%+ cure rate.

🔍 Myths vs Facts: Cervical Cancer Screening

❌

MYTH

"HPV vaccine means I don't need Pap smears"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

The vaccine protects against 70-90% of cancer-causing HPV types. You still need regular Pap smears to catch the remaining risk. Vaccine + screening = best protection.

❌

MYTH

"Only women with symptoms need screening"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Cervical cancer often has NO symptoms in early stages. By the time symptoms appear, cancer may be advanced. Regular screening detects precancerous changes before they become cancer.

❌

MYTH

"Pap smears hurt badly"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Most women feel mild discomfort for 5-10 seconds. Deep breathing helps. Inform your doctor if you're nervous—they can use smaller speculums or adjust technique. The brief discomfort is worth lifesaving prevention.

📊 Understanding Your Mammogram Results

✅

BI-RADS 1-2: Normal/Benign

No signs of cancer

Next Steps: Continue routine screening (annual or every 2 years based on age). Benign findings like cysts are common and not concerning.
⚠️

BI-RADS 3: Probably Benign

Follow-up recommended

Next Steps: Short-term follow-up mammogram in 6 months. <2% cancer risk. Most resolve or remain stable. May need ultrasound for better imaging.
🔴

BI-RADS 4-5: Suspicious

Biopsy needed

Next Steps: Biopsy required to determine if cancer is present. 20-95% cancer probability. Additional imaging (MRI, ultrasound). Early detection improves survival to 99% for Stage I.

🔍 Myths vs Facts: Breast Cancer Screening

❌

MYTH

"Mammograms cause breast cancer from radiation"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Radiation exposure is extremely low—equivalent to a few months of natural background radiation. Benefits of early detection far outweigh the minimal risk. Modern digital mammography uses even less radiation.

❌

MYTH

"Dense breasts mean I have cancer"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Breast density is just a physical characteristic—not a disease. Dense tissue can make mammograms harder to read. Your doctor may recommend supplemental ultrasound or MRI. Inform your doctor about density.

❌

MYTH

"I don't need screening—no family history"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Only 5-10% of breast cancers are hereditary. Most women with breast cancer have NO family history. Age and being female are the biggest risk factors. Everyone needs screening.

📊 Understanding Your Colonoscopy Results

✅

Normal / No Polyps

Clean colon, no abnormalities

Next Steps: Repeat in 10 years. Continue high-fiber diet, limit red meat, stay active. Excellent prognosis.
⚠️

Small Polyps Found

Polyps removed during procedure

Next Steps: Repeat colonoscopy in 3-5 years (depending on polyp type/size). Most polyps are benign. Removal prevents future cancer. Follow dietary recommendations.
🔴

Large/Cancerous Polyps

Biopsy indicates cancer/high-risk polyps

Next Steps: Oncology referral for treatment planning. CT scan to check spread. Surgery may be needed. When caught early, 90% 5-year survival rate.

🔍 Myths vs Facts: Colorectal Screening

❌

MYTH

"Colonoscopy prep is worse than the procedure"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Yes, prep is unpleasant—but it's just one day. The actual procedure is painless (you're sedated). New prep formulas are easier to tolerate. Alternative: Stool-based tests (FIT, Cologuard) have no prep but may miss polyps.

❌

MYTH

"I'm too young—it's an old person's disease"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Colorectal cancer rates are RISING in people under 50. Screening now starts at 45 (used to be 50). If you have family history or symptoms (bleeding, pain), start earlier. Don't ignore symptoms.

❌

MYTH

"Finding polyps means I have cancer"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Most polyps are NOT cancer—they're precancerous growths that take 10-15 years to become cancer. Finding and removing them PREVENTS cancer. This is why screening works so well!

📊 Understanding Your DEXA Scan Results

✅

T-score: -1.0 or above

Normal bone density

Next Steps: Continue weight-bearing exercise, calcium/vitamin D intake. Repeat scan based on risk factors (typically 2-5 years).
⚠️

T-score: -1.0 to -2.5

Osteopenia (low bone mass)

Next Steps: Increase calcium/vitamin D. Resistance training. Fall prevention. May need medication if high fracture risk. Repeat scan in 1-2 years.
🔴

T-score: -2.5 or below

Osteoporosis

Next Steps: Medication (bisphosphonates) likely needed. Physical therapy. Home safety assessment. Treat underlying causes. Repeat scan yearly.

🔍 Myths vs Facts: Bone Density

❌

MYTH

"Only elderly women get osteoporosis"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Peak bone mass is built by age 30. Low calcium intake, smoking, or early menopause can cause bone loss in younger women. Prevention starts early with exercise and nutrition.

📊 Understanding Your Glucose/HbA1c Results

✅

Fasting: <100 mg/dL | HbA1c: <5.7%

Normal blood sugar

Next Steps: Recheck every 3 years. Maintain healthy weight, balanced diet, regular exercise.
⚠️

Fasting: 100-125 | HbA1c: 5.7-6.4%

Prediabetes

Next Steps: Lifestyle changes critical! Lose 5-7% body weight. 150 min/week exercise. Recheck annually. Metformin may be recommended.
🔴

Fasting: ≥126 | HbA1c: ≥6.5%

Diabetes

Next Steps: Diabetes management program. Medication likely needed. Monitor blood sugar at home. Eye/kidney/foot exams. Dietitian referral.

🔍 Myths vs Facts: Diabetes

❌

MYTH

"Eating too much sugar causes diabetes"

Click to reveal truth →
✅

FACT

Type 2 diabetes is caused by insulin resistance—not just sugar. Risk factors: obesity, genetics, inactivity, PCOS. Sugar contributes to weight gain, but total diet quality and exercise matter more.
